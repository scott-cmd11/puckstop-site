How to Track Your Hockey Goalie's Stats

Tracking your goalie's games turns a blur of Saturday-morning hockey into a story you can actually see: what's improving, what to work on, and a record you'll be glad you kept. Here's how to do it simply — and keep it about growth, not grades.

Why track at all?

Goalies get remembered for goals, not saves. A stat record fixes that. Done right, it's a development journal: it shows a young goalie their progress, gives a coach real information, and gives you something better than "how'd you play?" on the car ride home. The goal isn't to grade a nine-year-old — it's to spot trends and celebrate growth.

What to track

Start with the essentials. You can always add detail later.

  • Shots against, saves, and goals — the foundation for save percentage and GAA.
  • By period — many goalies have a "slow start" or "tired third" pattern that only shows up when you split it out.
  • Shot location — glove vs. blocker, high vs. low, five-hole. A simple shot map reveals tendencies a raw save percentage never will.
  • The result and one good thing — jot down a single positive each game. It's the most valuable "stat" you'll keep.

Three ways to track — and their trade-offs

1. Pen and paper

Free and simple, but tally marks are easy to lose track of during a fast game, and adding up a season by hand is a chore. Fine for a single game; painful for a season.

2. A spreadsheet

Great for season totals and charts if you're comfortable with formulas. The catch: you can't realistically update a spreadsheet live in a cold rink with mittens on. You end up reconstructing the game afterward from memory — which is exactly when the details get lost.

3. A goalie stats app

The right app solves the one hard part: capturing shots live without looking away from the ice. Large, distinct shot-entry controls, instant save percentage, and automatic season stats. Look for one that's fast, works offline (rink Wi-Fi is a myth), and doesn't lock your data behind a subscription or a cloud account.

How to track during a fast game

  • Only track shots on goal. Ignore misses and posts — they're not saves or goals.
  • Tap first, detail later. Record the save or goal in the moment; add shot location only if you have a beat. Don't miss the next shot chasing detail on the last one.
  • Have an undo. You will mis-tap. A quick undo keeps the numbers honest.
  • Don't sweat perfection. Missing one shot in a 30-shot game barely moves the trend — which is all you're really after.

How to read the numbers (without overreacting)

The single most important habit: look at trends, not single games. Save percentage and GAA are noisy night to night — a couple of odd-man rushes can wreck a great performance. Over 10 games, the signal shows up. Watch for:

  • Save percentage drifting up across the season.
  • A shot map that's getting cleaner in a spot they've been working on.
  • Periods evening out (fewer "bad thirds").

Keep it about development

Numbers are a tool, not a verdict. Save percentage reflects shot quality, team defense, and the level of play — not just the goalie. Use the stats to have better conversations ("your glove side looked sharp tonight") and to notice progress your kid can't see game to game. A goalie who enjoys the position keeps playing it — and that's the stat that matters most.

Frequently asked questions

What stats should a beginner track?

Just shots, saves, and goals. That gives you save percentage and GAA. Add shot location and per-period splits once the basics feel easy.

Do I need an app, or is paper fine?

Paper works for one game. For a season — and for tracking live without missing shots — an app is far less painful and does the math for you.

How many games before the stats mean anything?

Treat single games as anecdotes. Patterns get trustworthy around 8–10 games.
